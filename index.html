Adaptive state-space filter for MetaTrader 5

It re-estimates its own model as the market moves, and notices within a few bars when that model has broken.

Most robots read indicators whose settings were chosen once and never change. KalmanFlow Pro estimates two things directly from your own chart data — where the price actually is, once the noise is removed, and how fast it is moving — and re-fits the parameters that control that estimate on a rolling window, by maximum likelihood, without you tuning anything.

On top of that sit two filters with deliberately different speeds: a change point test that reacts in a few bars when the price stops behaving like the model says it should, and a three-state regime model that decides how much size the conditions deserve.

No parameter you have to guess

The one number that governs how much the filter trusts the price against its own forecast is estimated from the data by maximum likelihood, on a rolling window, and updated daily. There is no period, no multiplier and no threshold for you to optimise into a curve fit.

It can decide not to trade

Two independent brakes. The change point test pauses new entries when the price stops fitting the model. The scoring layer keeps the robot flat whenever neither of its two behaviours has been earning its costs recently.

Costs are in the decision

Every signal is checked against the spread it would have to pay before it is allowed through. On instruments where the spread eats the expected move, the robot simply does not trade, which is the correct answer.

Three layers, three different speeds

Stacking three models on the same data and the same window gives you one overfitted model with three names. These three look at different things and update at different rates, which is the entire point of the design.

The filter, every bar

A local linear trend model runs on the logarithm of the price. It keeps two numbers: the level, which is the price with the noise taken out, and the slope, which is the rate of change per bar. Both come with an honest error bar, so the robot can ask whether a slope is large compared to its own uncertainty rather than compared to an arbitrary threshold.

Direction comes from here and only from here.

The change point test, every bar

When the model is correct, the difference between the price and the model's forecast is a standard normal sequence. So a cumulative sum test on that sequence is not a detector of large candles: it is a detector of the model itself breaking. It carries no window and needs no fitting, which is why it is the fast layer.

When it fires, the filter's uncertainty is widened so it re-locks onto the new level at once, and new entries pause.

What “it learns” actually means here

The phrase is used loosely by most vendors, so here is the precise version, with the numbers that were measured on the reference implementation rather than claimed.

The fit is walk-forward by construction

Every re-estimate reads closed bars only, and the window ends at the bar before the decision. There is no separate walk-forward harness to trust, because no other kind of run is possible: the robot cannot see the bar it is trading.

The estimate is checked, not assumed

With the fitted parameters the model's forecast errors came out with a standard deviation of 1.000 and a first-order autocorrelation of +0.011, which is what a correctly specified filter should produce. The likelihood has a single maximum across the whole search range, so the fit does not land somewhere different each time.

The break detector is calibrated

At its default setting the test raises one false alarm roughly every 510 bars, and detects a genuine two-sigma shift in about three bars, or one bar for a three-sigma shift. Both numbers move together: you can make it quieter or faster, not both.

And what it does not mean

Re-learning more often is not automatically better. A shorter window re-estimates with more variance, and that is overfitting under a friendlier name. The parameter fit here runs daily but over a long window, so it moves slowly and deliberately. The fast reaction is the change point test's job, not the fitting's.

Maximum likelihood also optimises one-step-ahead prediction of the price, which is not the same objective as making money. The filter being well specified is a necessary condition for the robot to work, not a sufficient one.

What a good backtest here does and does not prove

The re-learning is walk-forward by construction, so the robot never sees the bar it trades and a tester result is not contaminated by hindsight in that respect. What it still cannot tell you is how the same rules behave in a market regime that is not in your history, or how your broker fills orders in practice. Run it on demo before you run it with money.

Questions

Do I need to understand what a Kalman filter is?

No. You need to know that the robot estimates the price with the noise stripped out, that it re-fits its own settings from your data rather than from a preset, that it will stop trading when the price stops fitting its model, and that you set the risk. The mathematics is documented for people who want it and ignorable for people who do not.

What do I actually have to configure?

The risk percentage and the stop size in ATR. Everything the filter needs is estimated from the data. The remaining inputs exist so that you can test alternatives, not because they need tuning before it runs.

Will it work on my broker?

It runs on any MetaTrader 5 broker. Whether it is profitable there is a different question, and the spread decides it. Backtest on your own broker's data first: that is free and needs no licence.

Why is it flat so often?

Because it is allowed to be. The scoring layer keeps it out when neither of its behaviours has been covering its costs, and the change point test pauses entries after a break. A robot that is always in the market cannot express the conclusion that there is nothing to do.

Can I run it on several symbols?

Yes, one copy per chart, each with its own magic number. Remember that the risk percentage applies per copy, so three copies at one per cent is three per cent of exposure, not one.

Does it use martingale, grid or averaging down?

No. One position at a time, a stop loss on every trade from the moment it opens, and the size fixed by your risk percentage. It never adds to a losing position.

Is there a free trial?

The Strategy Tester runs without a licence, so you can test it as long as you like before buying. Live and demo accounts both need a key.
